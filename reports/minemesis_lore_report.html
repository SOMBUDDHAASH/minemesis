1. Executive Summary & The Problem of Communicating History

In deep emergent world simulations like Project Minemesis, the simulation continuously generates intricate historical narratives: tragic accidental deaths, generational blood feuds, bitter border raids, religious schisms, holy pilgrimages, and solemn peace armistices. However, a player or observer traversing a generated settlement cannot directly read the raw internal data tables. Without an in-world medium of transmission, the observer merely sees three buildings, a temple, and several silent villagers, completely oblivious to the fifty years of bloodshed and sacred concord that established the village.

Milestone L0 solves this fundamental communication barrier. Rather than relying on external user-interface logs or non-diegetic tooltips, history is materialized as physical, paginated written codices, manuscripts, and scrolls preserved inside settlement archives, temple repositories, and domestic hearth bookshelves. Crucially, this system operates with strict zero-LLM architecture: all texts are deterministically synthesized using combinatoric slot grammars, subjective psychological perspective transformers, vocabulary banks scaled by literacy level, and causal event graph links.

The Diegetic Interface Axiom: In Project Minemesis, books are not static decorative lore scattered across tables. They are active, subjective, dynamic views projected directly from the underlying Event Log, character beliefs, memories, relationships, and theological traditions.

6. Architecture Isolation & Deterministic Separation

To satisfy the strict Architecture Freeze and avoid perturbing the core simulation's random sequence, LoreEngine instantiates an isolated Pseudo-Random Number Generator keyed with the constant ASCII bitmask 0x4C4F5245 ("LORE"):

self.rng = random.Random(seed ^ 0x4C4F5245)

Furthermore, individual character literacy evaluations utilize independent secondary seed hashing: (self.seed << 16) ^ hash(char.id) ^ 0x51A7. This guarantees that literacy assignments and vocabulary selections remain completely invariant regardless of the order in which characters or settlements are queried.

3. SUBJECTIVITY & THE PERSPECTIVE BIAS ENGINE

9. The Non-Omniscience Axiom

A fatal flaw in many procedural generation systems is omniscient narration: books describe world events with perfect knowledge of behind-the-scenes variables, secret motives, and absolute causes. In Minemesis, characters do not have access to the simulation's global truth. They only perceive events through their own sensory range, distorted by cognitive suspicion, emotional grudges, and theological dogma.

The Perspective Transformation Law: A written document does not record what actually happened; it records what the author believes happened, expressed in the language of their social station and worldview.

10. Empirical Benchmark: Four Perspectives on an Accidental Death

To validate this architecture, a benchmark scenario was tested across four distinct authors recording the identical objective simulation event: Rowan Ironwood died in Year 4 during a timber-clearing accident at set_ironwood (Cause: Accidental tree fall; Zero malice).

Genre: Personal Journal | Literacy: BasicLiteracy | Suspicion: 0.85

Distortion Mechanics: High character suspicion (> 0.6) coupled with negative affinity transforms an accidental event into a perceived deliberate homicide, embedding an unverified grudge into family lore.

B. Devout Village Priest (Brother Arthur) Theological Dogma Bias

"In the grace of Year 4, Rowan was called home by the divine powers at set_ironwood, entering the realm of the ancestors and sacred spirits. The heavens called his spirit home while toiling upon the sacred earth. We lit consecrated torches in solemn prayer, honoring his passage into eternal rest."

Distortion Mechanics: The religious interpretation filter intercepts the mortality event, contextualizing tragedy as sacred fulfillment and cosmological destiny.

Genre: Historical Chronicle | Literacy: Historian | Era: Decadal Census

"The annals record that in Year 4, Rowan is recorded as having died at set_ironwood due to forestry accident. His passing concluded thirty years of documented agricultural expansion in the upper timber stands, marking a transitional census boundary for House Ironwood."

Distortion Mechanics: Cold chronological perspective prioritizing economic outputs, demographic milestones, and settlement labor force continuity over personal emotion.

27. The Multi-Tier Repository Infrastructure

Books in Minemesis are not stored in an amorphous global database. Every book is physically housed in a specific repository within a specific settlement. The LibraryManager maintains four distinct institutional repository types per settlement:

TownArchive (Civic Hall) Municipal Vault

Curator: Settlement Historian / Senior Scribe.
Housed Genres: HistoricalChronicle, LegalRecord.
Description: The official administrative repository of the town council. Stores decadal annals, census counts, diplomatic treaties, armistices, and judicial murder inquest verdicts.

TempleLibrary (Sacred Scriptorium) Ecclesiastical Sanctuary

Curator: Ordained Priest / Temple Monk.
Housed Genres: ReligiousText, MythCollection.
Description: Consecrated vaults attached to local temples and shrines. Guards foundational scriptures, theological commentaries, liturgical rites, and ancestral myth anthologies.

HouseholdShelf (Domestic Hearth) Private Kinship

Curator: Family Patriarch / Matriarch.
Housed Genres: FamilyHistory, PersonalJournal, LocalFolklore.
Description: Private domestic bookshelves kept near family hearths. Houses dynastic genealogies, intimate personal journals, grudges, and indigenous animist folk tales.

ScholarsStudy (Academy / Study) Academic Archive

Curator: Senior Scholar / Master Merchant.
Housed Genres: Biography, TravelAccount.
Description: Workrooms of traveling merchants and scholars. Stores monumental biographies of deceased chieftains, foreign caravan route travelogues, and pilgrimage memoirs.

28. Natural Genre Routing (`_default_library_for_genre`)

When a document is authored, the LibraryManager automatically determines its natural resting repository based on its genre taxonomy, ensuring realistic institutional segregation: 

def _default_library_for_genre(self, genre: BookGenre) -> LibraryType:
    if genre in (BookGenre.RELIGIOUS_TEXT, BookGenre.MYTH_COLLECTION):
        return LibraryType.TEMPLE_LIBRARY
    elif genre in (BookGenre.HISTORICAL_CHRONICLE, BookGenre.LEGAL_RECORD):
        return LibraryType.TOWN_ARCHIVE
    elif genre in (BookGenre.BIOGRAPHY, BookGenre.TRAVEL_ACCOUNT):
        return LibraryType.SCHOLARS_STUDY
    else:  # FAMILY_HISTORY, PERSONAL_JOURNAL, LOCAL_FOLKLORE
        return LibraryType.HOUSEHOLD_SHELF

29. Manuscript Physical State Tracking

Beyond text content, each manuscript maintains a physical state representation:

  • original_book_id: None for the original autograph manuscript; string ID if the book is a handwritten copy made by a scribe.
  • is_damaged: Boolean flag set to True if the book survived a fire or raid but suffered partial charring or torn leaves.
  • total_word_count: Exact word count reflecting surviving text after physical damage.

30. Cultural Memory Loss: The Destruction of Physical History

Human history is characterized not only by what was recorded, but by what was lost: the Library of Alexandria, the House of Wisdom in Baghdad, the Mayan codices. In Minemesis, written history is physically vulnerable. When a settlement suffers a military raid, siege, or collapse, its archives are exposed to looting, fire, and destruction.

The Cultural Amnesia Rule: When an archive is incinerated, the objective Event Log retains reality, but the settlement's written memory of that reality is permanently erased from the world.

31. Mathematical Archive Devastation Model (`damage_archive`)

When an EventType.RAID strikes a settlement, LoreEngine invokes damage_archive(settlement_id, damage_ratio=0.35, rng). Every book in each repository rolls deterministically against the damage threshold:

Total IncinerationLoss ≤ 0.175

Roll < damage_ratio * 0.5:
The codex is completely destroyed. The book object is purged from _all_books and the library collection. The text is lost to history forever.

Partial Charring0.175 ≤ Loss < 0.35

Roll < damage_ratio:
The codex is permanently damaged. The latter half of its pages are torn or burned away, replaced by "[... Parchment torn and blackened by fire ...]".

Intact SurvivalLoss ≥ 0.35

Roll ≥ damage_ratio:
The codex survives the incursion unscathed, hidden beneath rubble or protected by monastery vaults.
